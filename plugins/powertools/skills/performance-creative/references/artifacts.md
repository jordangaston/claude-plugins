## The insight statement

The output of Phase 0. One sentence. If it takes a paragraph, you haven't found it yet.

**Template:**

&gt; They aren't buying **[product/feature]**. They're buying **[the end of a feeling / the resolution of an identity gap]**.

**Test it against three criteria:**

1. Does it surprise someone on the team? (If everyone nods, it's a restatement of the value prop.)

2. Is it grounded in verbatim customer language you can point to?

3. Could you brief a creator from it without mentioning the product's features?

**Worked examples:**

- *They aren't buying non-alcoholic beer. They're buying back a ritual they gave up.*

- *They aren't buying a watch. They're buying a way to say something to a man who won't discuss it.*

- *They aren't buying skincare. They're buying the end of having seventy bottles on the counter.*

Note that none of these mention a feature, and each one implies a completely different ad.

## The concept brief

The output of Phase 1. One per position. Keep it to a page.

```markdown

# Concept: [short memorable name]

## Insight

[One sentence. From the insight statement.]

## Persona

- **Belief:** [what they hold true about themselves]

- **Emotion:** [what that belief produces]

- **Behavior:** [what the emotion drives them to do]

- **Their words:** ["verbatim quote from the corpus"]

## Position on the grid

- **Valence:** [high/low] × [positive/negative]

- **Self-concept anchor:** [Actual / Ideal / Ought]

- **Language intensity:** [low / high]

- **Awareness stage:** [Unaware → Most Aware]

## The stop

[What earns the first moment of attention. May be an image, a format,

a face, a first frame — not necessarily a headline.]

## The argument

[Why they should believe it. What evidence, in what order.]

## The ask

[CTA]

## What would make this fail

[Name the objection this concept does NOT handle.]

```

That last field matters. A concept that handles every objection is a concept that says nothing sharply.

## The UGC creator brief

The output of Phase 3. The brief *is* the leverage — roughly 1 in 5 creators works out, and that ratio is not fixable with a better marketplace, only with a tighter brief and more casting.

```markdown

# Brief: [Concept name] — [Creator name]

## Who you are in this video

[The persona, written so the creator knows how to *be* this person.

Not demographics. A situation and a feeling.]

## The one thing this video must communicate

[The insight. One sentence.]

## Hooks — use these, don't improvise

1. [Hook A]

2. [Hook B]

3. [Hook C]

## Beat sheet

- 0:00–0:03 — [the stop]

- 0:03–0:10 — [the problem, in your own words]

- 0:10–0:20 — [the demonstration / the turn]

- 0:20–0:30 — [the ask]

## Say this

[Specific phrases, ideally lifted from customer language]

## Never say this

[Brand jargon, feature lists, anything that reads as an ad]

## Shooting notes

- Vertical, phone, chest height

- Window light at 45°, no ring light

- Lav mic or phone close to mouth

- Ugly is fine. Polished is worse than ugly.

## Deliverables

- [N] takes of the full script

- [N] alternate hooks, shot separately so they can be cut in

- Raw footage

```

**Two rules to state plainly to the user:**

1. **Hand creators scripts and hooks.** Creators are reliably weak at hooks and don't know the brand. Save improvisation for creators who have proven out — then mine *them* for ideas, because they use the product.

2. **Cast across personas, not aesthetics.** Different ages, roles, races, backgrounds. Four creators representing four personas teaches more than four scripts read by one creator — and gives the algorithm four visually distinct ads rather than one clustered set.

## The content-analysis retro

The output of Phase 4. Run it after every meaningful test. The point is to end at a *content* claim, never at a number.

```markdown

# Retro: [test name], [date range]

## What won

[Ad name] — [primary KPIs: spend, results, CPA/ROAS]

## What the storytelling KPIs say

[CPM, hook rate, hold rate, CTR — each relative to account top performers,

never to industry benchmarks. What do they localize?]

## Content analysis — the actual work

- **Format:** [and did format matter, or is it incidental?]

- **Creator:** [how much of this is attributable to the person?]

- **Messaging:** [which strategy bucket? which awareness stage?]

- **Imagery:** [production quality, setting, POV]

- **Persona:** [who was this for, and did it reach them?]

## Why it won

[One paragraph. Specific enough to brief the next round.

"It had a better hook" is not an answer.]

## What we'd test next

- **Iteration:** [refresh plays for this winner]

- **Net-new:** [which unoccupied grid cell this points toward]

## What we still don't know

[The confound you accepted. Name it.]

```

**The discipline:** if the retro can be summarized as "ad 4 had the best CPA," it isn't a retro. It's a screenshot.

## The concept queue

Maintained continuously. The antidote to iteration paralysis.

| Concept | Grid cell | Persona | Status | Insight |

|---|---|---|---|---|

| | | | Queued / Testing / Won / Dead | |

**Rules:**

- Never let the queue empty. When a winner fatigues, you should be pulling, not scrambling.

- If everything in the queue occupies the same grid cell, you have a research problem, not a production problem. Return to Phase 0.

- Dead concepts stay in the table with a one-line note on *why*. The graveyard is the most valuable column, and it is the first thing a new team member should read.
