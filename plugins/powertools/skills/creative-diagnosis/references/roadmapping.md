## Where roadmapping sits

**Research → Roadmapping → Production.**

Roadmapping happens *after* research and *before* anything gets made. This is the step almost nobody teaches, and it's where the ideas get filtered.

The conversation the team has, every week: *here are all our amazing ideas — now filter them through the lens of what has the most evidence for performance.*

What survives the filter is a much smaller list. That list is the roadmap.

---

## The tier system

Every creative gets tiered, because tier determines cost, speed, and where it belongs in the plan.

| Tier | What it is | Speed |

|---|---|---|

| **Tier 1** | Edit / iteration | Days |

| **Tier 2** | Post-production | ~A week |

| **Tier 3** | Production / new content | Weeks |

**Tier 1 doesn't wait.** If an iteration can get out the door within a week, it goes into this week's sprint regardless of what the quarterly roadmap says. Every initial roadmap for a new brand should produce quick low-hanging-fruit wins in the first week or two, straight from the research.

## Evidence ranking

The filter. Every idea gets sorted before it gets scheduled.

### High confidence

- Formats, messaging, and creators you have **pressure-tested** and already see working

- Your own review data and persona research

- Your own past performance data

- A proven angle that hasn't been fully exploited

### Low confidence

- "It's our competitor's longest-running ad"

- "Because our competitors did it"

- "This is going viral on TikTok"

**Reject low-confidence evidence at the standup.** You do not know why a competitor's ad has been running a long time. It is frequently *lower funnel* — a long-lived ad buried in a retargeting campaign, which says nothing about cold acquisition.

**The standard to hold strategists to:** if the only evidence someone brings is runtime, with no reference to personas, no research, and no awareness-level filter, they are not doing the job.

### Applying it

For each idea in the icebox, ask literally: *do we have evidence this will work?* Yes or no.

&gt; "Before-and-after ads showing dry skin — is that something we have evidence for?"

If the answer is no, it goes back in the icebox. Not deleted. Not scheduled.

## The four-part roadmap

One document. Four sections.

### 1. Creators

List the creators you like and the creators you want to work with. Even in the age of AI, you still need creators, partnership ads, and real partners to get great content out the door.

### 2. The icebox

Every idea, written down, uncategorized, ungraded.

- *"I want to do a before/after of this."*

- *"This messaging point really sticks."*

- *"This persona is interesting."*

**Nobody is graded on what's in the icebox.** That's the point — it removes the cost of depositing an idea.

**For leaders:** a central location for ideas will save you repeatedly. The failure mode is arriving at the end of a sprint cycle and asking "what are the ideas?" — a question that should never need asking. Enforce continuous deposits. Sheet, Notion, whatever. The tool is irrelevant; the habit is everything.

## Quarterly planning

Map, a quarter ahead:

- Holidays and marketing-calendar events

- Product launches

- **Which personas you'll test in which part of the year**

- Business-as-usual iterations

**The persona-sequencing move.** After a first round of persona research, teams panic: *we have eight personas, how do we test them all?* You don't, not at once. Schedule them. A hot-climate gym-goer segment can wait until Q2, so you have room to explore other personas in depth first.

**Why this earns its keep.** Knowing the launches and the Mother's Days in advance means you aren't reinventing the wheel every time marketing lobs a request over the wall. It buys you space to do the actual job.

## The monthly roadmap

Built after creators are chosen, ideas are in the icebox, and the quarter is planned.

**The shape of each entry:**

| Field | Content |

|---|---|

| **Persona** | Which one this is for |

| **Idea** | One to two sentences |

| **Messaging options** | Three — hooks, or variations |

| **Tier** | 1 / 2 / 3 |

| **Evidence** | High or low confidence, and why |

Three messaging options per idea, not one. Not ten.

**Agency-side:** duplicate the roadmap monthly, so May and June are separately legible per client.

**Brand-side:** run one continuous roadmap forever, collapsing rows as things ship and get analyzed.

**The purpose of each part, restated:**

- **Icebox** — get everything documented. It saves you later.

- **Quarterly** — plan what's coming so you can prioritize what gets real results.

- **Monthly** — force clarity and better decisions.

## Sprint cadence

| Cadence | Ritual | Output |

|---|---|---|

| Monthly | Build the creative roadmap | Prioritized ideas |

| Weekly (Fri or Mon) | Standup | Which creatives move into production |

| Daily / bi-daily | Production standup | Unblocking |

| Monthly | **Creative retro** with team or client | What won, and why |

The retro closes the loop back into research. Without it, the roadmap becomes a production queue rather than a learning system.

## The thing to keep repeating

The value is not in the template. It's in the act.

&gt; **The sheer act of writing down your ideas and prioritizing them will put you ahead of 75% of creative strategists.**

Most brands — including very large ones — react week to week. Filtering ideas through an evidence lens, deciding what's on the chopping block and what goes into production, is what makes someone strategic. There is no secret code beyond this.

And when the temptation arrives to lead with the most creative idea in the room:

&gt; **It is not your job to be creative. It is your job to drive revenue.**

Most of the time, that means taking advantage of the rich data you already have.
